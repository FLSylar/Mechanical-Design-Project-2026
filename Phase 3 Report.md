Concrete list of design changes:
Shaft with blades
Scaled down the entire part to fit skateboard bearings by 1:0.133333, made blades thicker with respect to the shaft, reduced number of blades to six to accommodate new thickness and also fillets around the shaft, modified shaft to fit bearing (8mm), chamfer shaft so bearings can rotate freely without interference.

Turbine housing
Scaled down part by 1:0.1362069 to accommodate the shaft and blades, made the bottom support thicker to ensure structural integrity of the prototype, modified the structure for final assembly, split the housing into the main housing and the top support. 

Top support 
Scaled down by 1:0.1362069 to ensure all components fit together. Modified the radius to fit standard skateboard bearings in prototype (22 mm)


Assembly procedure and challenges:
The fan blades printed in about 20 hours and set aside. The turbine housing was printed in about 34 hours and set aside. The support was printed in about an hour and a half. First, the skateboard bearings were press fit with a small hammer into the shaft. Then the shaft with bearings was hammered into the housing. We tried tapping the top support to secure everything but there was too much resistance. So, we used a file to file down the support ends so that it could pressfit easier without being too loose.


Test procedures, results, and interpretation:
We tested how well our turbine spun by flicking lightly with our finger. It spun very well thanks to the skateboard bearings used. A more forceful flick gets it spinning for quite a while. Blowing into the waterway also gets it to spin which means water should make the turbine spin for power generation.


Comparison with Phase 2 predictions (where applicable):
The only thing we could physically test from phase 2 was the turbine rotation which worked really well thanks to the skateboard bearings and overall geometry. We could not physically test the turbine strength or fatigue in comparison to the ansys simulations since the materials and geometry changed significantly for the prototype.


Detailed discussion of failures, mistakes, and surprises:
First shaft and blade print failed due to using low infill density while pressing the prototype together, however, second reprint had much higher infill leading to a successful assembly. Also, we had to sand down the top support to get it to press fit correctly. This was important in order to secure the turbine shaft securely.


Concrete list of design changes you would implement in a second iteration:
Resized the top support to fit properly the first time.
Consider rusting of the bearing as they are metal.
See if we can make the blades hollow for less mass used.
